VirtGaze · Computer vision · Hands-free interaction

VirtGaze: A plain webcam, steered by your eyes. 

VirtGaze estimates where you are looking in 3D and moves the desktop cursor there, using nothing but a standard webcam and a short calibration.

The problem

A webcam sees a head, not a gaze.

Gaze is a tiny signal riding on a large one. The iris moves a few pixels while the head moves freely, the lighting changes, and every face and camera is different. A cursor also has to feel immediate, so accuracy cannot be bought with lag.

That rules out a single end-to-end model. VirtGaze splits the job: geometry handles what physics already explains, a small model learns what is personal to you, and a filter keeps the result steady without making it sluggish.

The approach

Eight stages, one thread each.

Every stage runs on its own thread, joined by bounded queues, so a slow frame is dropped rather than queued up as latency.

  1. 01 / 08

    Capture

    Webcam or video input, with optional lens undistortion from a chessboard calibration.

  2. 02 / 08

    Detect and track

    Full face detection every few frames, with tracking in between to save time.

  3. 03 / 08

    Face mesh and iris

    MediaPipe landmarks with iris refinement give the eye geometry.

  4. 04 / 08

    Head pose

    solvePnP against a canonical face model recovers rotation and position.

  5. 05 / 08

    3D gaze

    Gaze rays are built per eye in the head frame, then rotated into the camera frame.

  6. 06 / 08

    Personal MLP

    A small per-user network maps gaze features to the screen, through PyTorch, ONNX Runtime or TensorRT.

  7. 07 / 08

    Temporal filter

    An unscented Kalman filter and One Euro filter, tuned by fixation, saccade and blink state.

  8. 08 / 08

    Screen coordinates

    A per-user bias map removes location-dependent error before the cursor moves.

Try it

Same eye, calmer cursor.

Raw gaze estimates jitter even when the eye holds still. A One Euro filter smooths heavily at low speed and lets go at high speed, so fixations stay steady and jumps stay fast. Move your pointer across the panel.

Key decisions

Where the attention went.

  • Two coordinate frames, on purpose

    Model features stay in the head frame, which makes the learned mapping invariant to head pose. The geometric fallback uses camera-frame angles. Mixing them silently breaks calibration, so the split is documented and tested.

  • Backpressure over buffering

    Bounded queues between threads give natural backpressure. Under load the pipeline sheds old frames, because a stale gaze point is worth less than a missing one.

  • Smoothing that follows the eye

    Filter noise scales with gaze state: heavier during fixation and blinks, lighter during saccades. The cursor rests steadily and still moves quickly when you look away.

  • No cursor beats a wrong cursor

    Without a usable personal model the system can fall back to geometry, or be set to hold still. A plausible but uncalibrated point is the failure this option exists to prevent.

  • Calibration that keeps learning

    A full 25-point session trains the model, a 5-point pass refreshes it, and implicit mode treats deliberate clicks as labelled samples and fine-tunes in the background.

  • Targets are stated as targets

    The accuracy and latency figures are design targets. Benchmark scripts ship with the repo so anyone can measure them on their own camera, lighting and hardware.
