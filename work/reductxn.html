The problem

Every payment costs something.

Paying each debt directly means more transfers than a group needs, and each transfer can carry a tax or a fee. The common trick, largest debtor pays largest creditor, is not minimal, and it cannot tell you how far from minimal it is.

Reductxn treats this as what it is: a partition problem over balances. Money moves outside the app and people mark payments done, so the product’s whole job is to be exactly right about who owes whom.

Condensation

Many debts, condensed.

Scroll to run settle-up on a sample group of eight people. The animation plays a real result, computed by a port of the engine’s exact search.

5payments settle everyone

  1. 01

    Everyone records

    Twenty debts between eight people. Paying each one directly would take twenty transfers.

  2. 02

    Debts become balances

    Only each person’s net position matters. Every line collapses into one number, and the numbers sum to zero.

  3. 03

    Find the zero-sum groups

    The fewest payments is n − k, where k is the most disjoint groups that each sum to zero. Finding k is NP-hard, so the engine solves it exactly with dynamic programming over subsets.

  4. 04

    Pay the fewest

    Each group of s people settles in s − 1 payments. Eight people, three groups, five payments: proven fewest, not just a good guess.

Formally verified in Lean 4

“Proven fewest” rests on one theorem, and Lean 4 has checked it: no plan can settle a group in fewer than n − k payments.

How it works

Now in the real app.

A six-person trip with eight shared expenses, from the first typed line to the last payment.

  1. 01

    Say what happened

    Type one line. Reductxn reads the amount, who paid and who was in, and shows you before anything is saved.

  2. 02

    Or scan the bill

    Photograph a receipt and it’s read on your device, line by line. You check every line and fix anything it got wrong before saving.

  3. 03

    See where you stand

    Every expense lands in the ledger, and each person’s balance updates. They always add up to zero.

  4. 04

    Follow the money

    The Flow view draws who paid for whom, and shows how tangled a group’s debts have become.

  5. 05

    Settle in the fewest payments

    Fourteen direct payments become five. The app can say “Proven fewest”, because the search is exact.

  6. 06

    Pay, then mark it done

    Money moves outside the app, by UPI, bank transfer or cash. Mark it paid, and the person paid confirms it.

The approach

One cycle, closing at zero.

Every expense takes the same path through a modular Spring Boot service, and the books balance at each step.

  1. 01

    Record

    An expense, IOU or payment comes in, online or queued on the device while offline. Each create carries an Idempotency-Key.

  2. 02

    Split

    Shares use the largest-remainder method in integer minor units, so they add up to the total exactly.

  3. 03

    Post

    It becomes ledger lines of “debtor owes creditor amount”, with a gapless sequence number per group.

  4. 04

    Project

    Balances are projections written in the same transaction, so they always add up to zero.

  5. 05

    Settle

    The engine finds the fewest payments, then the cheapest of those plans using sourced tax and fee rules.

  6. 06

    Mark paid

    Money moves outside the app. People mark payments done, the ledger records them, and balances return to zero.

Key decisions

Posted, and never edited.

  1. #001

    Provably fewest, and honest above that

    Up to 20 people the plan is exact, and tests compare it with an independent brute force on hundreds of random groups. Larger groups use a greedy plan and say when it is not proven optimal.

  2. #002

    The ledger cannot be rewritten

    Database triggers refuse UPDATE, DELETE and TRUNCATE on ledger tables. Edits and undo append reversing entries, so every number can be explained.

  3. #003

    Money is never a float

    Amounts are integers in the currency’s minor unit, and splits use the largest-remainder method, so nobody gains or loses a cent to rounding.

  4. #004

    Costs are sourced, or marked unknown

    Every tax and fee rule cites a source URL and the date it was checked. A case with no rule shows as unknown, because a confident wrong number is worse than none.

  5. #005

    Every write is safe to retry

    Creates take an Idempotency-Key and updates need If-Match, answering 428 if it is missing and 412 if it is stale. Double taps and flaky networks can’t double-post.

  6. #006

    Built for bad signal

    Trips are where groups spend most and signal is worst. The app opens from what it saved, and changes wait on the device to be sent once, in order, when the connection returns.

  7. #007

    One service, enforced modules

    Modules call each other only through their api package and each owns one schema. ArchUnit and an SQL ownership test fail the build otherwise.

  8. #008

    Server and web agree by test

    Money is computed on both sides, so split maths, conversions and activity entries are pinned in shared fixtures that each side must reproduce to the cent.
